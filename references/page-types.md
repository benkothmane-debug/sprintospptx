# Step 3 — The three page registers

Every slide is **Analytical**, **Written (prose)**, or **Visual**. Form follows **the idea**, not an imposed template.

> Deck language: rules apply whether the deck is in French or in English. French examples are kept verbatim as French-deck illustrations, with English-deck equivalents alongside.

## Form follows the idea (do NOT put everything in number boxes)
- The idea is **quantitative** (a number proves the point) → **Analytical** (one exhibit + a short reading).
- The idea is **qualitative / argumentative / explanatory** (a mechanism, a cause, a stake, a nuance) → **Written**: a real **paragraph** or a developed argument, not a decorative chart or telegraphic bullets.
- The idea is a **strong moment** (statement, transition, quote, structure) → **Visual** (crafted dark background).

> ⚠️ Anti-reflex: **do not turn every slide into KPI cards or "chart + 3 bullets"**. A chart that merely repeats its own numbers, surrounded by 3 bullets, makes a **thin** slide. If the point is an analysis, **write it out**.
>
> ⚠️ Anti-monotony: **vary the registers and layouts** across the deck. A well-made working deck alternates written pages, exhibits, and a few visual pages; it does not repeat the same exhibit+bullets template 12 times.

## "Consulting-grade" density = a COMPOSED multi-zone slide (the reflex to acquire)
Density done right is neither "one exhibit + 3 bullets" nor half-empty prose: it is a **composed slide** that **fills the canvas** with **2 to 4 coordinated zones** (+ a "Key takeaway" band only when it adds a so-what beyond the title) (e.g. framework + table + dots; waterfall + table; bars + donut; table + scatter). In consulting firms, **several exhibits on one slide** are the norm. **Catalog of 60 dense patterns (BCG/MBB) + components (harvey balls, dots, metric cards, waterfall, 2×2, issue tree, Gantt) → `references/slide-layouts.md`.** Pick a pattern from the catalog rather than re-improvising a "chart + bullets".

## Density by deck type (reading register)
- **Deck for oral presentation**: leaner slides, the presenter carries the detail (depth goes in the notes).
- **Working / reading / brief deck** (framing note, country brief, dossier): the slide **must stand on its own**: it carries **real written paragraphs**, not just titles and 3 bullets. This is the default case whenever the deliverable will be **read** without a presenter.
Choose the register early (see archetype) and commit to it: an economic brief gets read, so it gets written.

## A) ANALYTICAL page (consulting-firm style)

**Light background** (white), gray/black, **a single accent color**, dense, sober. 6-block format:
1. **Eyebrow** (small mono/gray caps: EN "Pillar 2 · Profitability" / FR « Pilier 2 · Rentabilité »).
2. **Action title** (conclusion sentence, black, bold) + thin accent rule below.
3. **Key message** (one gray line under the title).
4. **Main exhibit** (a single one, center/left) — see library below.
5. **Proof points** (2-4 self-contained sentences, to the right of or below the exhibit).
6. **Implication (so-what)** as a bottom band + **sources/assumptions** in the footer + page number.

**Exhibit library** (one per slide, the most telling):
- **Comparison table** (FY-1/FY/var.), key column highlighted.
- **Columns / bars** (trajectory, mix): the key bar in accent, the others in gray.
- **Benchmark** (rows = criteria, columns = options).
- **Waterfall / bridge** (cash, margin, revenue bridge): floating bars + dotted connectors.
- **2×2 matrix** (probability × impact, attractiveness × feasibility): positioned points + labels.
- **Logic tree / issue tree**, **funnel**, **timeline**.
- **Pyramid** (governing idea + 3 pillars) for the executive summary.

Rules: a single exhibit; annotate the key point; no generic image or useless icon; numbers up front.

---

## C) WRITTEN page (prose / working slide)

**Light background**, same frame (eyebrow, action title, rule, implication, sources) but the core is **written text**, not an exhibit. Use when the point is an analysis, an explanation, a line of reasoning, a piece of context.

Templates:
- **Opening paragraph + development**: a **kicker** (2-3 sentences that set up the idea), then 3-5 **developed sub-points** (each = a bold thesis sentence followed by 1-3 sentences of explanation). ≥ 180 words, written out, not telegraphic; develop as much as the analysis requires (global ceiling: 1,500 words/slide, see wording.md).
- **Two columns of prose**: e.g. "Finding" / "Why it matters", or "Trend" / "Risk"; each column a real paragraph.
- **Text + small supporting exhibit**: 60% written text on the left, one **small** chart/number on the right **in support** (the inverse of the analytical page, where the exhibit dominates).
- **Definition / explainer**: term + clear definition + concrete example, in sentences.
- **Narrative**: a short story/argument that flows (context → tension → implication).

Writing rules (see `wording.md`): **complete sentences**, formal but clear tone, **put the numbers inside the sentence** (EN: "growth rose from 3.8% in 2024 to roughly 4.7% expected in 2025" / FR: « la croissance est passée de 3,8 % en 2024 à environ 4,7 % attendus en 2025 »), no fragmented bullets and no `[F]/[I]` tags scattered across the slide (sources go in the footer, labels in the notes if needed). Keep a comfortable reading measure (≈ 80-95 characters/line, airy line spacing).

> A well-made written page is worth more than three "chart + bullets" slides: it is often **the** slide that makes people say "now that is a real working slide".

---

## B) VISUAL page (premium, crafted dark background)

For the cover, the agenda, the dividers, strong statements, the closing. **Generated dark background** (gradient + halo + shapes, see `structure-and-color.md`), large type, **one idea**, single accent.

Principles (inherited from the retained design skills):
- **Weight inversion**: hero title rather light, section titles heavier.
- **Tracked mono eyebrow** above the title (EN "NVIDIA · INVESTOR RELATIONS" / FR « NVIDIA · RELATIONS INVESTISSEURS »).
- **Flat cards with sharp edges** (no large rounding, no "glow" shadow).
- **Never pure black/white**; **a single accent**; light off-white text.
- **Anti-slop**: no purple gradient, no glassmorphism, no gadget icons.
- Same **action-title** method as the analytical pages (the title concludes).

Typical visual templates:
- **Cover**: eyebrow + large title + governing idea (1 sentence) + metadata block (audience · date · confidential) + source.
- **Agenda**: eyebrow (EN "Agenda" / FR « Sommaire ») + action title + Objective band + 6 numbered sections (accent number, clear label, gray description, page reference).
- **Divider**: part number + section title + one framing sentence.
- **Closing**: "In summary" (FR « En synthèse ») + bottom line (1 sentence) + 3 next steps + contact + source.

---

## Consistency
Same margins, same accent, same fonts, same footers across the whole deck. The light↔dark alternation follows the **sandwich**: structure = dark, analytical content = light (details in `structure-and-color.md`).
